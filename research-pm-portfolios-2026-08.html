The State of PM Portfolios

What trending product-manager portfolios look like right now: 25+ real examples with links, seven live-site deep-dives, what hiring managers say separates strong from mediocre, and the format shifts of 2024–2026.

4 parallel research streams 13 primary sources fetched 7 portfolios verified live

01 · TL;DR

What the research says in five points

  • Portfolios are still rare, which makes them a differentiator. Surveys by Aakash Gupta (325 PMs, 15 hiring managers) put global adoption at ~16–18% — roughly 10% in the Americas, ~20% in Europe, and ~40% in Asia/Middle East, where Indian job postings sometimes request them outright.
  • Depth beats volume, and reasoning beats polish. Every hiring-side source converges on the same message: 2–3 deep case studies showing decisions, trade-offs, and quantified outcomes beat five shallow ones or a beautiful site with no decision trail. A mediocre portfolio actively hurts more than having none.
  • The 2025–26 format shift is from static Notion docs to "vibe-coded" personal sites with live demos — PMs (especially AI PM candidates) building their own custom sites and shipping working AI prototypes as proof they build, not just describe.
  • The strongest differentiators observed on live sites are voice and a singular artifact — a distinct persona (Sri Ponnada), or one vivid technical project (Justin Hinh's autonomous coin-sorting robot) — not generic corporate polish.
  • There is no canonical "famous PM portfolio." Unlike design, no single PM portfolio is universally cited; roundups mostly point to templates and platforms. The field is open for anyone who does it well.

03 · Deep dives

Seven live portfolios, dissected

These were fetched and analyzed directly, not taken from roundup descriptions.

Nicolas Backal

Director of Product Design, Okta · nicolasbackal.com

Structure: Home → Selected Writing → Selected Work → Product Cookbook → Talks → Contact. Case studies are deliberately compact: hero image, headline, role/timeline, a 2–3 sentence narrative, "learn more" link — scannability over exhaustive teardown ("Okta Personal: zero to one", "end-user redesign impacting 30M+ users").

Seniority is proven through curation and cross-functional artifacts (the co-authored Product Cookbook series, ADPList mentorship), not case-study volume.

Sri Ponnada

PM at a big tech co + stand-up comedian · sriponnada.com

Structure: About Me → My (Actual) Work → My (Other) Work → Let's Chat. The parenthetical labels are the whole personality hook, deliberately blurring career and creative life. Conversational copy ("Hey! I'm Sri 👋🏽"), minimal visual flourish.

Distinctiveness can come entirely from voice and copy rather than structure — a model for candidates leaning on personal brand over deep case studies.

Justin Hinh

AI Digital PM (Fannie Mae, Cognizant) · justinhinh.webflow.io

Structure: Portfolio / LinkedIn / Blog / Resume / Contact with project cards, category tags, and thumbnails. Flagship piece: "Numi v4: Autonomous AI Coin Sorting, Rebuilt From Scratch" — a hardware/AI robot he actually built.

One vivid, technical passion project can outweigh a stack of generic corporate case studies for signaling "builds with AI, not just describes it." This is the archetype of the 2025–26 AI-PM portfolio.

Sam Dickie

PM & maker for early-stage founders · samdickie.me

Structure: About / Now / Writing / Projects / Downloads / Newsletter plus service tags (No-Code, MVPs, Customer Development). Case studies are replaced by articles and an "Office Hours" consulting track — 108 past clients as social proof, a Notion template pack for sale, live AI experiments documented.

The portfolio doubles as a lead-gen funnel — the right model for builder/consultant hybrids, less so for pure job-seekers.

Dr. Yuliya Rubtsova

PM / Data Scientist, PhD in NLP · mokoron.com

Structure: About → Work (Selected Projects, Academic Research, Public Speaking, Side Projects) → Mentoring → Resume → Contact. Lightweight project cards ("AI-powered reading tutor for children") linking out for detail; muted palette, icon-driven skills.

A genuinely rare PM + ML-researcher credential carries the site — but surface pages are light on quantified outcomes, showing that even strong portfolios often under-deliver on on-page storytelling.

Kane Sherwell

Senior / freelance PM · kanesherwell.com

Structure: About / Works / "Book Now" (Calendly) in header and footer. Work is a scrolling grid of client cards linking to live sites; animated gradient-heavy agency polish; tech-stack carousels; hard numbers (500+ products shipped, 100% Upwork success, 400% viewership growth).

Optimized to convert freelance leads, not persuade hiring managers — case-study depth sacrificed for breadth and CTAs. Know which audience your portfolio serves.

Luis Jurado

AI/ML PM & agile thought leader · luisjurado.me

Structure: A content hub (Product Management / Machine Learning / Data / Blog / Portfolio) that works like a personal magazine, with a daily tech-news podcast and an "emotional intelligence in PM" positioning.

Cautionary lesson: with portfolio work secondary to thought-leadership content and no testimonials, it risks reading as blogger rather than practitioner with shipped work.

04 · What makes them distinct

Cross-cutting patterns from the live sites

  • Voice and a singular artifact beat polish. The most memorable sites won on persona (Ponnada, Dickie) or one unusual concrete thing (Hinh's robot, Jurado's podcast, Backal's Product Cookbook) — not on template quality.
  • The common weakness, even among standouts: cards without narrative. Most sites show project cards with minimal on-page story and push depth to external links. Full problem → approach → metrics storytelling on the page itself is rare — which means doing it well is an open differentiator.
  • Platform tracks positioning. Squarespace minimalism dominates among senior/corporate PMs; Webflow animation and CTA-heavy design dominates among freelance/maker-positioned PMs; Notion dominates among speed-focused and junior PMs.
  • Curation signals seniority. The senior sites (Backal) show fewer, tighter pieces; junior sites tend to show everything. "Selected Work" as a label is itself a signal.

05 · The hiring-manager view

What actually gets evaluated

  • Quantified, causal impact — not activity. "Shipped X → activation up Y%" with specific numbers ("15% increase in WAU", "22% reduction in drop-off"), never "improved engagement." (Aakash Gupta)
  • Decision-making over output. Hiring managers want the why: trade-offs, constraints, prioritization frameworks. "Most portfolios focus on 'what we built' instead of 'why we built it that way' and 'what happened next.'" (Product Manager Job Board)
  • A resume tells what you did; a portfolio shows how you think — the core value proposition, echoed by hiring-side commenters on Blind.
  • Tailoring to the target role separates strong from generic — work samples should map to problems the hiring team actually has.
  • Originality is a hard gate. Tutorial-derived or templated case-study work reads worse than having nothing.

Anti-patterns repeatedly called out

  • Feature lists with vague ownership ("contributed to…") — reads as padding.
  • Generic teardowns with no original insight ("10 ways to fix Spotify's onboarding").
  • Slick visuals with no decision trail — designer-format, PM-empty.
  • NDA overreach — sharing work you can't openly explain; skip it rather than fudge it.
  • Too many case studies — quality over quantity, consistently.
  • Misaligned seniority framing — juniors overreaching into "vision-setting" language; seniors underselling strategic scope.
The asymmetry to remember: a good portfolio only helps, but a mediocre one actively hurts — multiple sources agree the real decision is depth of effort, not whether to have one.

Expert-recommended case-study skeletons

Three frameworks from independent sources converge on the same arc:

Context → Problem → Constraints → Process & trade-offs → Solution (your part) → Results + lessons

  • Product Manager Job Board's six-part structure (above): context and timeline, the challenge with data, real-world constraints, decision-making and trade-off analysis, what shipped and your specific contribution, metrics moved plus lessons learned.
  • STAR / PR-FAQ framing (Aakash Gupta): Situation → Task → Action → Result, with Amazon's PR/FAQ format for strategy-heavy pieces; show work-in-progress thinking and named frameworks, not only the polished end state.
  • Shawli's build process: pick which skills to showcase calibrated to your level → sketch structure before building → collect and sanitize real artifacts (spreadsheets, screenshots, models) → explicitly narrate constraints and choices → digitize.

Who needs one most

Consensus: portfolios are most load-bearing at transition points — career switchers, APM/entry-level candidates without a PM track record, and people re-entering after a layoff or gap. Experienced PMs with strong track records are frequently hired without one. India stands out: portfolio requests appear directly in job postings.

06 · Trends 2024–2026

Where the format is heading

  1. From static docs to "vibe-coded" live sites. The clearest 2026 trend: PMs using AI tools (Lovable, Claude Code, Base44) to build custom sites in hours — explicitly positioned as proof of shipping. Aakash Gupta frames tiered build plans (30-minute / 8-hour / 40-hour versions).
  2. Distribution matters as much as the site: the recommended trio is open web (SEO on your name), resume link, and LinkedIn Featured section — the last claimed to drive the majority of mentee offers.
  3. Notion remains the fast default but is seen as aesthetically limited — praised for text-first, database-driven case studies that mirror how PMs already document (embed Figma, Loom, project databases), criticized for rarely looking as polished as a coded site.
  4. Platform tiering by career stage: Notion/templates for speed and junior PMs; Squarespace for mid-career polish; Webflow for senior candidates wanting custom branding; PM-specific tools (UXfolio, portfolio.dev-style builders with viewer analytics) emerging.
  5. Content structure has converged on the problem → process → quantified-outcome narrative, with "the why behind every decision" as the explicit centerpiece.
  6. Embedded artifacts are increasingly expected: Figma prototypes, Loom walkthroughs, Miro boards — and for the AI era, live embedded demos of things the candidate actually built.
  7. AI-PM content is its own sub-genre: weekend-buildable side projects (working AI apps, agent demos, dashboards) as portfolio centerpieces, proving technical fluency a resume can't.
  8. Product-sense interviews are moving earlier and AI prototypes are becoming table stakes in 2025–26 hiring cycles (Duolingo, Stripe, LTK cited) — the portfolio is drifting from artifact to pre-interview screen.
