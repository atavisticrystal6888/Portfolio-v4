## Who it's for, and why

Aarchid is for plant enthusiasts and collectors, the people who own enough plants that one dying quietly in a corner is a real loss. Their job is to catch a problem early and know what to do about it.

The apps they already use, Planta, PictureThis and Greg, lean on static care calendars and species lookups. None correlate what the leaf in front of you looks like with the environmental history that produced it, and none cite a source you could check.

The gap: a forensic, auditable diagnosis that looks at your plant, factors in your local conditions, and recommends an action grounded in botanical research, not a template.

Aarchid is a two-person side project built with Dilpreet Grover (@dfordp). I owned product scope, the golden eval set and the citation-grounding requirement; platform engineering was shared.

> Source: aarchid-rework (no longer public — it holds the Workers orchestrator and the eval harness described below) · [aarchid-api](https://github.com/dfordp/aarchid-api) (the public data-layer API only; it does not contain the diagnosis pipeline) · live at [aarchid.space](https://www.aarchid.space/).

## What I learned before building

I interviewed 10+ plant enthusiasts about failure modes and where they had stopped trusting an app, and benchmarked the top 4 apps on diagnostic specificity, citation quality and edge-case handling. Three things changed the plan:

- **The trust gap is a sourcing gap.** Users could not check why an app said what it said, so every recommendation read as a guess.
- **Diagnosis has to be visual and local.** A species lookup answers "what is this plant". The real question was "what is wrong with this one, here".
- **Citations are the differentiator.** None of the benchmarked apps cite sources.

## The bets, and what we cut

Three constraints were fixed before the first line of code.

- **Latency.** A diagnosis that arrives after the user has closed the app is worthless. P95 under 10 s end to end, photo upload included.
- **Cost ceiling.** A two-person side project cannot carry idle server bills. Fixed infrastructure in single-digit dollars a month, per-user cost in cents.
- **Auditability.** An AI that prescribes treatment for a living thing has to show its sources. Every recommendation needed a citation a user could follow, not a confident guess.

## What we built

It starts with a photo.

**Forensic Health Audit.** Gemini 1.5 Pro reads the photo for pests, deficiencies and stress. In parallel, the Exa AI API pulls peer-reviewed citations for the symptoms and species, and OpenWeather adds 7 days of local context. Back comes a health score from 1 to 100, a severity tier (Healthy, Warning or Critical) and an action plan with a citation on each recommendation.

**Growth Velocity Tracking.** Over weeks of photo logs, pixel-based measurement tracks leaf expansion, stem elongation and internodal distance. Species-specific benchmarks turn that into a verdict: thriving, stagnating or declining.

**Pro-sumer Asset Management.** For collectors with 50+ plants, care actions can be batched by micro-climate zone. Exportable, timestamped Health Certificates give a plant verifiable provenance for peer-to-peer sales. This is the business wedge.

<Decision>

The most consequential call was keeping the orchestrator stateless, with persistence in Supabase (history, growth tracking) and R2 (images). The Worker is cheap to re-run and trivial to version, and the eval harness can replay any historical photo against a new model without touching user data.

</Decision>

## How we knew it worked

The eval set is 200 labelled samples across 12 common plant-health failure modes. Every model or prompt change re-runs the whole set and reports the accuracy delta, so prompt iteration went from vibes to measurable, with a result in minutes.

Aarchid is live at [aarchid.space](https://www.aarchid.space/). The limits of the evidence: accuracy is measured on our golden set, not on live user photos, and with no confirm-or-correct loop yet, field accuracy is not measured. Usage figures are not reported here.

## Outcome

Product:

- **Diagnosis accuracy:** 92% on a 200-sample golden set covering 12 failure modes
- **Citation grounding:** 90% of recommendations carry a peer-reviewed source link
- **Latency:** P95 under 10 s end to end on Cloudflare's edge
- **Unit economics:** ~$0.25 per active user per month at scale; sub-$5 a month fixed infra at low usage
- **Live:** [aarchid.space](https://www.aarchid.space/), co-built with [Dilpreet Grover](https://github.com/dfordp)

Build:

- Two-person side project, Aug 2025 to present, with a replayable eval harness

## What I'd do next

- **Next bet:** a structured feedback loop. Users confirm or correct a diagnosis and the deltas feed back into the golden set. The harness exists; the pipe is the next PR.
- **Open question:** whether Health Certificates are the wedge we think they are. The provenance story is built; whether sellers pay for it is not measured yet.
- **What I'd do differently:** build the correction loop alongside the harness. The 12 failure modes are the ones we chose; users will find the ones we did not.

<UnderTheHood title="Architecture and implementation">

### The Edge Stack

Next.js PWA → Cloudflare Workers (Hono) orchestrator → Gemini 1.5 Pro (vision) + Exa AI API (research) + OpenWeather (environment) → Supabase (Postgres) + Cloudflare R2 (image storage, zero egress).

### Request flow

Image upload lands on R2 (zero egress). A single Worker fans out three parallel calls, Gemini vision, Exa research and OpenWeather (7-day local context), then composes the response. No cold starts, under 50 ms overhead, global by default. Exa returns peer-reviewed citations inline; the frontend renders them as a footnote trail.
